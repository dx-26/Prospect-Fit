# Assignment 4A Reflection

The biggest change between my Cowork GRASP brief and my Claude Code GRASP brief was the level of precision. In the Cowork stage, my goal was broad because I needed the agent to gather and organize useful background for a fictional workforce-training prospect. The Resources section allowed open-web research for general manufacturing trends, while the Autonomy limits focused on preventing contact, private-data access, and unsupported claims. My sign-off happened when I reviewed the completed research packet and confirmed that every company-specific detail was clearly marked as fictional, assumed, or unknown.

The Code stage required much more exact instructions. I identified the input filename, file format, app files, scoring categories, score thresholds, and required browser behaviors. Proof also changed from reviewing the quality of a research packet to running specific functional tests. The form had to calculate a score from 0 to 100, update every category, handle missing information, remain clear and responsive in the Professional Navy & Teal design, reset correctly, and keep qualification disabled until a person completed the sign-off checkbox. This made sense because code needs repeatable rules instead of general judgment.

One moment when I had to correct the agent involved incomplete records. A first scoring idea could have calculated a percentage using only answered questions. That approach would make a prospect with one strong answer look more qualified than it really was. I corrected the model so every unanswered category contributes zero while the total possible score always stays at 100. I also kept the scoring categories visible so a user can understand why the total changed instead of trusting a unexplained number.

In a real business setting, I would trust this tool to run unsupervised for basic calculations, category displays, and consistent recommendations based on the entered answers. I would not trust it to make a final sales decision, reject a lead, contact a prospect, or treat assumptions as facts. The weights are not based on real historical outcomes, and a salesperson may know important context that the form cannot capture. For that reason, the human-review checkbox is a real sign-off point, not just a visual feature. The tool should support professional judgment rather than replace it.
